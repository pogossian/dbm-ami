The Lantern on the Cliff

Leah pulled her hood tighter as the wind pushed against her. She and her cousin Sam had been walking along the coastal path for nearly an hour, carrying a small box of medicine to the lighthouse keeper. The storm had damaged the road, so the narrow cliff path was the only route left.

“We should turn back if the rain gets heavier,” Sam said, glancing at the dark clouds gathering over the sea.

Leah shook her head. The keeper was expecting the medicine before nightfall. Although her legs ached, she continued carefully over the wet stones. The waves below crashed against the cliffs like enormous drums.

A few minutes later, a thick mist rolled across the path. Sam stopped. “I can’t see the next marker.”

Leah remembered the map in her pocket. It showed that the path curved inland beside an old stone wall before reaching the lighthouse. She searched slowly until her fingers touched the rough wall. “Stay close to this,” she said. “It should lead us away from the edge.”

They followed the wall until a pale yellow light appeared through the mist. At first it looked no bigger than a star. Then the lighthouse rose in front of them, tall and bright. The keeper opened the door with a relieved smile.

Leah handed him the medicine. Her hands were cold and her boots were soaked, but she felt proud. Outside, the storm still roared. Inside the lighthouse, the warm room felt as safe as a sheltering cave.

🔎 Evidence Detective — What does “give evidence” mean?

Evidence is proof from the text. It is a word, phrase, sentence or detail that shows your answer is true.

1. Answer the question
→
2. Find proof in the text
→
3. Explain the connection
Question: How do we know Leah was determined?
Answer only: “Leah was determined.” ❌ This gives no proof.
Answer + evidence: “Leah was determined because although her legs ached, she continued.” ✅
Students may copy a short phrase from the text OR explain the detail in their own words.
Useful sentence starters: “I know this because…” • “The text says…” • “This is shown when…” • “For example,…”
